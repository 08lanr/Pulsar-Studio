# Harborlight producer simulation

A bounded fictional case at `/producer/simulation`, accessible from Company profile / My titles navigation. Company facts, budgets, concepts, scripts and development judgments are invented. No videos, measured performance, licensing deals or operational catalog records are created. Existing company data is untouched.

## Persona and job
Chen Lin, development lead at a small Hangzhou producer, must recommend one of three English-language concepts for a two-week development sprint. Her founder controls production approval. A US story editor and line producer are necessary collaborators, not existing capabilities implied by the product.

The page includes audience intent, distribution route, team capabilities, resource gaps, invented budget ceilings, format assumptions and commercial unknowns. Three concepts include a premise, production implication, failure risk, validation task and two short AI-authored original English episodes. These are opening scene samples, not timed, production-ready episodes or complete series scripts.

## Walkthrough
1. Read the company constraints before looking at market examples.
2. Compare The Exit Clause, The Last Receipt and Before Midnight, Again.
3. Open each script. Identify the heroine's agency, episode-ending question and expensive or confusing story devices.
4. Open the corresponding market search in a separate tab. It searches existing public catalog metadata, without pretending the fictional company is the active account or changing its profile filters.
5. Select one development candidate, record the rationale and a condition that could reverse it, then download the brief. Per-concept notes survive switching candidates within the page, but not reload/navigation. There is no operational greenlight or backend persistence.

## What this exposes
The Exit Clause is an illustrative first candidate because its contained scenes match the invented company's constraints. That is a production-fit judgment, not market validation or a hit forecast. The other two concepts stress location costs and story-rule clarity respectively.

A title detail needs to answer: what story device is visible; where can it be used in my script; what evidence supports the observation; what is inferred; what constraint changes the recommendation; and what action should happen next?

Next product iteration should prototype three actions against this case:
- Attach a comparable to a specific slate title, with an observation and source date. Keep public counters distinct from audience geography and causal explanations.
- Draft a script experiment: alternative opening, heroine goal, cliffhanger or localization question, with cited input and an editable hypothesis.
- Build a decision brief combining production constraints, evidence, unknowns and assigned validation tasks. Track whether a hypothesis was tested, rather than assigning unsupported success scores.

Do not interpret a synopsis, a single chart snapshot or raw platform counters as proof of US demand, profitability, retention or buyer appetite. None of those are supplied by this fictional exercise. The experiment deliberately surfaces the missing evidence.
