# The crazydramas connection (read-only)

Decision 2026-09-23 (top of `docs/decisions.md`), plan `connection-segment-plan.md` part A (A0–A5, A8). Studio reads the public crazydramas.com API and tells, for each title, where its series stands there, and never claims more than a read can prove. Phase 3a wrote nothing on crazydramas and held no credential; since phase 5 (decision 2026-09-23 "Upload to crazydramas", the last section below) Studio also reads the authenticated Studio API with `CRAZYDRAMAS_STUDIO_TOKEN` and, behind a live-write gate, creates draft series, uploads episodes and publishes them.

## What it answers, and what it never claims

For each Studio title, four things:

1. Is this series on crazydramas?
2. How many episodes are there, and are they numbered 1..N?
3. Which episodes match Studio's files, and which are missing, another length, or still processing?
4. What are the free/paid split, the price, whether an IAP product is set, and is the poster still the placeholder?

A duration match proves only **"same length"**. It never proves "same file". A true "identical" needs a record of what Studio itself uploaded — the ledger `studio.cd_publications` of phase 5: an episode reads `identical` only when a verified or published ledger row carries the file's own SHA-256 and its Mux length passes the frame rule. The series live today (Mafia King, Reclaiming Her World, He Hated All Women, …) were uploaded by hand through the crazydramas CMS, so for them the answer stays at "same length". `local_newer` exists in the types and is still never produced.

## Where the data comes from

Two public endpoints, read by `lib/crazydramas/transport.ts` and nothing else:

| Read | What it gives | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/dramas` | the published catalog | `Cookie: pulsar_mock=0` is sent **and** every `mock-*` slug is dropped after the parse, because the edge cache ignores the cookie and production mixes in 18 mock series. `cache: 'no-store'` (Next 14 caches server fetches otherwise). Up to 60 s of lag after a publish (public `max-age=60`). |
| `GET /api/dramas/<slug>` | the drama (id, status, freeEpisodeCount, seriesPriceCents, iapProductId, posterUrl, posterBlurhash) and its **published** episodes (episodeNumber, durationSeconds, status, isPublished) | **404 means "not live".** It does not mean "not uploaded": a draft and an archived series answer 404 too, and the public API cannot tell them apart. |

`playbackId`, `previewPlaybackId` and `thumbnailUrl` are **never stored or shown**: the thumbnail URL carries the Mux playback id of paid episodes (a paywall leak on crazydramas). The zod schemas in `lib/crazydramas/types.ts` are whitelists — nothing outside the fields named there survives a parse, and a hand-built row that carries one of those keys is refused. The saved bodies under `tests/fixtures/crazydramas/` are stripped the same way.

`CRAZYDRAMAS_BASE_URL` (default `https://crazydramas.com`) is the site's origin, not a secret. Fixture mode never fetches: `lib/crazydramas/fake.ts` answers with a deterministic catalog that mirrors `tests/fixtures/workspace` (`fixture-film` complete, its three episodes 120, 150 and 180 frames at 30 fps as `ffprobe -count_packets` measures the checked-in files; `-partial`, `-differs`, `-processing`, `-draft` (404), `-broken` (a failed read); plus the three real unmatched series), and its posters are two SVGs the app serves itself (`public/crazydramas-fake/poster.svg`, `poster-placeholder.svg`), so the browser never asks crazydramas.com for a poster either — the e2e spec aborts and fails on any request to another host. `CRAZYDRAMAS_LIVE_READ=1` is the engineer's override that allows the public reads from fixture mode. Under node:test the live transport refuses before any request, whatever the environment says. `lib/crazydramas/pick.ts` is where the transport is chosen; `index.ts` re-exports it, and no module inside the folder imports `./index`. (What broke `next build` under the default heap was not that cycle but output tracing following `path.join(process.cwd(), ".uploads")` in `lib/data/storage.ts` and `lib/data/launch.ts` into the runtime folder — 33 GB, some ten thousand entries; `next.config.js` now keeps `.uploads`, `.uploads-e2e` and `tmp` out of the trace with `outputFileTracingIgnores` / `outputFileTracingExcludes`.)

## What is kept (migration `0017_crazydramas_link.sql`)

- **`core.platform_links`** — which drama on crazydramas the title *is*: `title_id`, `platform 'crazydramas'`, `slug` (the slug the drama is read under now; a followed CMS rename moves it), `title_slug` (the title's own `crazydramas_slug` when the link was made or last re-pointed in Studio; a followed rename keeps it, or records the title's current slug when that is one the link already accepted — film-meta brought up to date with an earlier rename), `cd_drama_id`, `linked_at`, `linked_by`. Made on the first 200 read of `core.titles.crazydramas_slug` with the drama id the platform returned; from then on the title is matched by that id, because a slug can be edited in the crazydramas CMS. One link per title × platform, one title per drama id.
- **`core.platform_snapshots`** — append-only, one row per public read of one slug: `http_status`, the whitelisted `drama`, the `episodes` list, `read_at`, `error`; `title_id` the title the check ran for, null for a series that matches no title. The sweep keeps the last twenty per slug. These are public facts: producers of a title read its rows (`can_read_title`); a row with no title is staff's to see. A title's reading is built from **its own** rows of the slug ("one film is one title per company", so two companies' titles can carry one slug and each records its own reads); another title's rows are never this title's reading, whoever looks. Future stats (revenue, funnels) do **not** go here.

No job kind: a public GET costs no model money, `studio.jobs.target_id` is NOT NULL, and producers cannot read jobs. The snapshot row is the record. Both backends implement the rows through `getData()` (`getPlatformLink`, `listPlatformLinks`, `upsertPlatformLink`, `recordPlatformSnapshot`, `listPlatformSnapshots`, `listLatestPlatformSnapshots`, `prunePlatformSnapshots`, `listTitlesWithPlatformSlug`, `listTitleEpisodes`) with the same refusals: a foreign title is not found, a producer write is forbidden, a bad slug or body is invalid, a drama id already linked to another title is a conflict.

## The states

`lib/crazydramas/match.ts` derives the reading from what the data layer holds (the title, its episode rows, the slug's snapshots newest first, the link); it is never stored. The screens call one pure function, `crazydramasStatusFor(title, episodes, snapshots, link)`, and the loaders `loadCrazydramasStatus` / `loadCrazydramasStatuses` in `lib/crazydramas/sweep.ts` gather the inputs.

**Series state**, first rule that applies:

| State | When |
| --- | --- |
| `not_linked` | no slug in film-meta / on the title |
| `not_checked` | a slug, and no read yet (the sweep runs on the first tick after boot; an import checks at once) |
| `read_failed` | the newest read failed; the newest good read is shown, marked stale, with its own read time |
| `not_live` | 404 — "not uploaded, or draft". Also a 200 for **another** drama under the linked slug (`note: slug_reassigned`, a CMS edit). With the authenticated read (phase 5) the status says which: `detail: not_uploaded` (a 404 there), `draft` or `archived` (the series is seen and is not published) |
| `live_partial` | a Studio episode number is absent on crazydramas, or an episode there is still uploading, processing or failed |
| `live_differs` | an episode is another length, crazydramas has an episode Studio does not, or its numbers are not 1..N |
| `local_newer` | only with a ledger (plan A6): Studio's file is not the one it uploaded — never in v1 |
| `live_unverified` | every episode is there and ready, but Studio has no frame count for one of them (an uploaded, non-imported title) |
| `live_complete` | counts equal, numbers 1..N, every episode ready and "same length" — or "close", an older render; when any episode reads close the chip says so in words ("complete · some older renders", `chipReading().older`), never as a count |

Since phase 5 a snapshot records which read made it (`read_via`: `studio` = the authenticated `GET /api/studio/series/:series`, `public`), and the status carries it with `detail` (above) and the series' `managed_by` (`studio` / `cms`, null from a public read). The state names did not change: a published series is still judged on the episodes viewers see (an unpublished one reads missing), a draft's episodes are matched but the state stays `not_live`. The chips' words live in the screens.

`not_checked` and `live_unverified` are the two states plan A3 left implicit; both are honest answers rather than a claim. Keeping "close" inside `live_complete` is deliberate: Mafia King's 21 re-rendered episodes are one frame short of the zip's files and are not re-rendered (build amendment 2), and a series that reads "lengths differ" would also be swept every 15 minutes for good.

**Per-episode verdict**, pairing by episode number: `missing` (Studio has it, crazydramas does not), `extra` (crazydramas has it, Studio does not), `not_ready` (status uploading, processing or failed, or no length yet), then the frame rule below: `same_length`, `close`, `different_length`, `unknown` (Studio has no frame count), and `identical` (ledger only).

**Free/paid and extras:** free is episodes 1..freeEpisodeCount, paid the rest; the price is `seriesPriceCents` ($9.99 today); "IAP set" is whether `iapProductId` is present; the poster is the placeholder when its URL contains `-placeholder` (three live series today: he-mocked-…, he-treated-…, ever-since-…).

## The frame rule and its calibration caveat

```
d = round(durationSeconds × fps) − video_frames
```

with `durationSeconds` from crazydramas (what Mux reports), `fps` from the file (inferred from the import's own `video_frames` / `duration_ms`, snapped to a standard rate; a caller may pass the film's fps) and `video_frames` from Studio's own ffprobe count on the imported link (`lib/film-import/import.ts`).

| d | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **+2** | `same_length` | the measured Mux offset: all 52 Mafia King files from `cut/eps.zip` give exactly +2 |
| +1, +3, +4 | `close` | the current re-renders show +3 and +4 against live: "probably an older render" |
| anything else | `different_length` | for example He Hated All Women's episodes 3, 4, 29, 30, 56 and 57 after their three boundary fixes were rendered |

**Never ±0.1 s**: it misfires on 69 of 169 live episodes (a file 0.000 s apart that is not the Mux offset is *not* the same length). The offset lives in one named constant, `MUX_FRAME_OFFSET = 2` (`lib/crazydramas/match.ts`, with `FRAME_RULE.calibrated_on`, `calibrated_files` and `confirmed: false`). **It is calibrated on one film.** It must be confirmed on the first Studio-made upload before it may block anything; until then the reading informs and never gates.

## Refresh

- **The sweep** — step 5 of the scheduler's tick (`lib/tiktok/scheduler.ts` → `tickCrazydramas()` in `lib/crazydramas/sweep.ts`): once an hour, or every 15 minutes while any title is `live_partial` or `live_differs` (an episode not ready reads partial) or the last sweep hit an error. One catalog GET plus one GET per linked or catalog slug, in sequence; the catalog's series that match no title are read and recorded with no title (the staff mirror's "Unmatched on crazydramas" list); then the prune to twenty rows per slug. Skipped under `SCHEDULER_DISABLED=1` and under node:test.
- **Check now** — `POST /api/titles/[id]/crazydramas/check`: same-origin guard, staff or a producer reviewer / approver (a viewer stays read-only and gets 403 from the route and from `checkCrazydramasTitle` alike, the rule the page's button follows), zod, the same check function, JSON. Refused with 429 (`code: too_soon`, `retry_after_s`, `Retry-After`) while a snapshot of that slug is younger than 30 seconds — before any request is made, and judged on every read of the slug whoever made it — and with 409 (`code: not_linked`) when the title has no slug. A foreign title is 404. The status in the answer is the caller's own reading (their session's rows of the title).
- **After an import** — `lib/film-import/import.ts` runs one check once a successful import set a slug, failure-soft (a failed check is a log line, never a failed import).
- **The staff mirror** — `GET /api/admin/crazydramas/unmatched` (and the Import films desk): the series live on crazydramas that match no Studio title, from the newest title-less snapshot per slug; `series` is null until a sweep has read the catalog at all.

Following a rename (`resolveReadSlug`): a check reads the link's slug; when the catalog lists the linked drama id under another slug (a CMS rename), that slug is read and the link's `slug` follows while its `title_slug` stays; when the title's own slug is none of the link's `title_slug`, its `slug` or the platform's (the person re-pointed the title in Studio), that slug is read and the link moves to the drama it answers with, `title_slug` becoming that slug — refused, in the snapshot's error and without naming the other title (it may be another company's: "…is a series linked to a different title, so the link was not moved; staff can resolve it"), when another title holds that drama. `title_slug` is what keeps the third check after a rename reading the platform's slug: film-meta still carries the pre-rename slug, and without the record of what the title's slug was at link time that read as a re-point (the phase 3a review). On a followed rename the link records the title's current slug as `title_slug` when it is already the link's `title_slug` or `slug` (film-meta was brought up to date with the first rename), so a second rename does not turn that slug into a re-point (round two). The screens read the same way (`loadCrazydramasStatus` takes `resolveReadSlug` without a catalog): after a re-point the reading is the title's own slug with no link — "not checked yet" until the new slug is read, "not live" when it answers 404 — never the old drama with a frozen checked time; and a check's answer carries the link only when the slug it read is the link's.

## The credential

One credential, `CRAZYDRAMAS_STUDIO_TOKEN` (phase 5). Like any later one it is always prefixed `CRAZYDRAMAS_`, never `NEXT_PUBLIC_` (crazydramas's own `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` / `_ANON_KEY` / `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` collide with Studio's), kept in `.env.local`, read only in `lib/crazydramas/transport.ts`, and never written to a row, a log, a fixture or a response — routes say "configured: yes/no" only (the `lib/tiktok/tokens.ts` rule).

The token reads `GET /api/studio/series/:series` (drafts and archived series, every episode published or not, `managed_by`); the snapshot keeps only the same whitelisted fields as a public read plus `managed_by` — never an episode's `playback_id` or Mux ids (those stay with the ledger Studio wrote itself). Without the token, or when crazydramas refuses it (401 / 403 / 503), the public read stands in. Still to come: the stats views (`funnel_by_drama`, `funnel_by_creative`) into a staff-only table, not the snapshots; and a Mux **Read** token (`CRAZYDRAMAS_MUX_TOKEN_ID` / `_SECRET`) for `GET /video/v1/assets/{id}/input-info`, the only retroactive identity check for the series uploaded through the CMS.

## Upload to crazydramas (phase 5)

Decision 2026-09-23 "Upload to crazydramas" (top of `docs/decisions.md`; Ruobin's spec, plan A6). The contract is crazydramas' `docs/STUDIO_API.md` on its `main` (PR #1 and Jayden's follow-ups: ownership, product ids, the title guard); Studio's side is `lib/crazydramas/publish.ts` (the flow and the uploader), `studio-client.ts` (one method per Studio API route, the contract's shapes as zod whitelists), `transport.ts` (the only module that reads the token and sends bytes), `ledger.ts` (the ledger's rules for both backends) and `fake.ts` (a whole crazydramas in memory for fixture mode and the tests).

### The flow

On the title's crazydramas section (the screens are phase 5's other half):

1. **Series details → Create draft series** — `PUT /api/titles/[id]/crazydramas/series`. The form starts from the title (display title, logline as tagline, synopsis as description, genre, language from the source locale), five free episodes, 999 cents, `cd.series.<short_name>` from the slug (at most 40 characters) and the poster from the title's cover; the slug Studio picked, the drafted tagline, description and genres and the poster Studio hosts are "Upload automation" below. The slug is the title's own `crazydramas_slug` (film-meta), or the link's once linked, read as the phase 3a section reads it (`resolveReadSlug`); the link's series is read by its drama id, so a CMS rename of its slug is the same series, updated under its new slug. A title re-pointed in Studio (its slug edited while a link exists) follows the slug it names now, with no link, only away from a series that is not a show on crazydramas yet — a Studio draft holding nothing but the title's own uploads (the typo fix): its series write creates or claims that series and moves the link there. A title linked to a series made in the CMS, a published one, or one holding anyone else's episodes (a show handed over to Studio) stays on its link: the section shows that series, `can_write` is false, and every write is refused `409 repointed` with `existing` before any write is sent — Studio never makes a second series of a show already on the site, nor moves the title off it (Put the slug back, a button under the locked slug that the slug route takes while locked, or ask staff). A title without one is `not_linked`. Before the first PUT Studio checks the show is not already live under another name — the working-name table of STUDIO_API.md (`KNOWN_LIVE_SERIES`) and the public catalog, by the title, the film's folder name and the name sent — and refuses with `series_title_exists` (and `existing`) rather than create a second paid series; crazydramas' own guard is the backstop for identical titles. A poster must answer 200 `image/*` before it is sent (`poster_unreachable`). Every series Studio creates is a **draft**, `managed_by: studio`, `cta_mode: web_checkout`; the link records its drama id and `managed_by`.
2. **Upload episodes** — `POST …/uploads` with a list or `all`. Each episode becomes a ledger row (`planned`) and a background runner uploads them one at a time per title, at most two titles at once on the machine. **Uploading never publishes.** An episode is skipped, with its reason, when Studio has no imported file for it (only the local-tier link is read), when that very file is already on crazydramas or already verified (while the episode still holds that asset: a verified or published row whose asset the fresh read no longer shows on the episode is `superseded` and proves nothing), when an older file of it is still uploading, or when crazydramas already holds media for it and `replace` was not asked. `POST …/uploads/cancel` stops one episode or all.
3. **Publish** — `POST …/publish` with the explicit episode list (and `publish_series` the first time). Only episodes Studio uploaded **and verified**, whose verified asset the fresh read still shows on the episode (`not_verified` otherwise: a replace since, Studio's or a CMS re-upload, is not what Studio verified). An episode past the series' free count is paid: until `CRAZYDRAMAS_PAYWALL_LIVE=1` it needs `confirm_paid` (`paid_needs_confirm` with `paid`), because paid episodes can be streamed free until the paywall fix is live on crazydramas. crazydramas' partial `episodes_changed` is recorded for what did go live and answered as that same 409. `POST …/unpublish` hides episodes (and sets a published series back to draft); their ledger rows go back to `verified`.
4. **Replace** (a re-cut) — the same upload with `replace: true`, which crazydramas accepts only while its episode is ready or errored. The viewer-impact warning comes first: the episode keeps playing the old cut until the new asset is ready, watch positions then refer to the old timing, and the old asset is kept (its id is recorded as `previous_asset_id`; Studio never deletes a Mux asset). A replace of an episode that is **already published** goes live when its asset is ready (crazydramas keeps `is_published`), before Studio's verification — the Replace dialog says so, and a failed check means unpublishing it; the ledger says `published` then, instead of pretending a publish is still to come. Once crazydramas answers `replaced: true` the episode's older verified or published rows are `superseded` at once. After someone else's upload has taken the episode (`taken_over`, `taken_over_late`, `replace_required`, or `upload_in_progress` when a Replace met a CMS upload still in flight) a Retry never inherits the row's old `replace`: only a person's fresh Replace sends Studio's file over that media, as a new upload (the dead one kept as `previous_upload_id`), and the screen offers Replace instead of Retry for such a row.

`GET …/publish` answers the section's poll: `series_state` (`not_linked | not_uploaded | draft | published | cms_managed | linked_elsewhere`), the series (the contract's fields), the form's defaults, per episode `{n, studio_frames, ledger_step, cd_status, is_published, is_free, verdict, bytes_sent, bytes_total, error, error_code, duration_s, replace_needed}`, `writes_enabled` (and `writes_disabled_reason`, the missing setting by name), `paywall_live`, `uploading`, and `can_write` (whoever reads the title may look; the buttons are the title's approver's or a staff administrator's). In live mode it reuses one authenticated read of the series for 20 seconds, so a two-second poll does not become thirty requests a minute on crazydramas.

### The per-episode uploader and the ledger

`studio.cd_publications` (migration 0019; `docs/data-model.md` section 11) holds one row per title × episode × file, key `cd:<title_id>:ep<k>:<sha8>`, one active row per episode. Each step is persisted **before** the next external call, and each pass of `advanceCdPublication` claims the row (CAS on `revision`, a ten-minute lease renewed by every write; a stale lease is adopted and the row resumes from its persisted step):

| Step | What happens next |
| --- | --- |
| `planned` | `attempted_at` written, the series read (taken back into the CMS: the row fails `series_not_studio` and nothing is sent), then `POST …/episodes/<n>/upload {sha256, bytes, frames, replace?}`. The upload id (and a replace's `previous_asset_id`) is recorded before any byte. A lost answer is settled by repeating the call: the same sha answers the same upload (`reused`). `webhook_pending` → sync, then repeat. `episode_busy` waits two minutes; 5xx, `mux_error`, `episode_changed`, `conflict_retry` and no answer wait with backoff (at most eight times, then `failed`); every other code fails the row with crazydramas' words. |
| `upload_created` | `GET /uploads/<id>` first, never a second upload: `asset_created` → the bytes are all there; errored / cancelled / timed out → a new upload with `replace: true` (the contract's remedy; `previous_upload_id` keeps the dead one) only while the episode still holds Studio's own dead upload — someone else's upload there now fails the row `replace_required`, whatever its `replace` says; `episode_is_current: false` → cancel ours, `taken_over`. The URL is never stored (it is a capability): after a restart the same upload call hands it back, sent **without** `replace` (it only asks for the URL of an upload known to be waiting; if that upload died meanwhile crazydramas answers `replace_required` and the dead upload goes the way above, never an upload the ledger does not know). The file is re-hashed from the local-tier link (`file_changed` if it is not the planned file), the empty status PUT asks the storage where it stands, and chunks of 16 MiB (a multiple of 256 KiB) follow from the **acknowledged** offset — the 308's `Range`, not where the last chunk ended, since the storage may persist fewer bytes than sent. `bytes_acked` follows each chunk. **Immediately before the last chunk** `GET /uploads/<id>` again: if a CMS upload has taken the episode over, the last chunk is not sent, Studio's upload is cancelled and the row fails `taken_over` ("tell the operator"). The series is read there too (`GET /series/<id>`): if it was taken back into the CMS (`managed_by: cms`) the last chunk is not sent and the row fails `series_not_studio` — nothing more goes to that series, not even a cancel; the unfinished upload expires within the hour. A person's Stop is honoured here too. |
| `bytes_sent` | Poll `GET /uploads/<id>` until `ready` (every 7 s inside a runner's pass; a row not ready yet waits for the next pass). `webhook_pending` for a minute → `POST …/sync`. `episode_is_current: false` now → `taken_over_late`: the bytes are in Mux, Studio neither syncs nor publishes and says so. An errored asset → `asset_errored`. |
| `asset_ready` | **Verify**: `asset.meta.external_id` must equal the file's sha256, and `d = round(duration × fps) − frames` must be exactly `MUX_FRAME_OFFSET` (+2). Anything else → `verify_failed` with the numbers and the calibration caveat; a failed verification is never published. Before the row is marked verified the series is read again: taken back into the CMS since, the row fails `series_not_studio` and is never verified. |
| `verified` | Ready to publish; an older row of the episode is `superseded`. |
| `published` | By the explicit publish (or a replace of a published episode, above). |

Mux direct-upload resume semantics were checked on 2026-09-23 against Mux's "Upload files directly" guide and Google Cloud Storage's resumable-upload guide (the upload URL is a GCS session); the citation is in `lib/crazydramas/transport.ts`.

The runner (`runTitleUploads`) is fire-and-forget from the uploads route (`scheduleCrazydramasUploads`, as the clip cutter's), one per title per process; the scheduler's tick (`tickCrazydramas`) also wakes every title with an active row no live lease holds (`resumeCrazydramasUploads`), so a restart or a dead worker resumes by itself. At most two titles send bytes at once on the machine: a runner reserves its slot before it waits on anything (so runners that start together, as after a restart, never all see a free one) and counts other processes' live leases, never its own process's twice. A worker whose row was adopted by another stops at its next write, even if that worker released it since; a later claim picks the row up, and an unexpected failure fails a row only while its worker still holds it. A row another live worker holds (another Studio server on the shared database, or this server's previous process within its lease) is left to it, and while that worker sends one of the title's episodes no other starts here; a pass that moves nothing, a lost claim included, sleeps before the next look. `CRAZYDRAMAS_UPLOADS=off` disables both; under node:test they run only when a test asks.

### The gates

- **Live writes** (spec §6): a write — PUT, POST, a Mux chunk — reaches crazydramas.com only with `DATA_SOURCE=supabase` **and** `CRAZYDRAMAS_LIVE_WRITES=enabled` **and** the token (`studioWriteRefusal()` names the missing one; the screens show it as `writes_disabled_reason`). Fixture mode writes to the fake only; fixture mode with the engineer's `CRAZYDRAMAS_LIVE_READ=1` reads the live site and writes nowhere. Under node:test the live transport refuses before any request, whatever the environment says.
- **Studio's series only** (spec §5): every series made in the CMS (`managed_by: cms`, all eight live before Studio) is shown read-only — "made in the CMS — ask Jayden to hand it over (one SQL line) to manage it from Studio" — and every write to it is refused **before any write is sent** (`series_not_studio`): from the link's `managed_by` once an authenticated read recorded it (then with no call at all), else from the fresh read that precedes every write. The uploader reads the series (`GET /series/<id>`) before each write it sends — the upload call, a sync, a resume's URL re-request, a cancel — and before the last chunk and the `verified` save, so after a take-back the title's other queued episodes send nothing either: each row fails `series_not_studio`.
- **One title per series**: two companies' titles can carry one slug ("one film is one title per company"); the series is the title's whose link holds its drama id. To any other title it is foreign, as a foreign title is not found: `series_state: linked_elsewhere` with nothing of the series (no fields, episodes or form values from it), every write refused `409 conflict` before any write is sent, in words that name no other title, and that title's check keeps only what the public read shows of the series (nothing of a draft, no `managed_by`). Uploads, publishes and unpublishes go only to the series the title's own link holds (`409 series_unlinked`: save the series details first); the series write alone claims a series, after its checks for holders. A Studio series no link holds (an orphan, left when a title's link moved off it) may be shown to and claimed by a title only while all it holds is that title's own uploads (an empty draft included); holding anyone else's episodes, it is foreign to that title as above.
- **One series per title's ledger**: the ledger's key is title × episode × file, so while a title's rows belong to another series than the one it writes to (it was re-pointed after uploading to the old one, or that series is gone) every write is refused `409 repointed` before anything is sent — Put the slug back (the same button), or staff resolves it — and the section shows none of the old series' rows.
- **The poster check** asks only a host whose every resolved address is public (no loopback, private, CGNAT, link-local or unique-local address), so an approver's poster address never makes Studio's server ask something on its own network.
- **Paid episodes** (spec §3): the extra confirm until `CRAZYDRAMAS_PAYWALL_LIVE=1`.
- **Who**: the title's approver or a staff administrator; a foreign title is not found.
- **The token**: read in one function of `transport.ts`, sent in one header, never logged, stored, returned or put in an error (a test runs a whole upload with a sentinel token and searches every answer, row, refusal and console line for it). An upload URL is treated the same way; no playback id is stored or shown.

### Upload automation: poster, slug, series text

Decision 2026-09-23 "Upload automation: poster, slug, series text" (top of `docs/decisions.md`), after Ruobin's rule: "Studio does routine steps itself — never tell the user to edit a JSON file, run Update, or paste SQL for routine work; every routine value gets a sensible automatic default plus an in-page way to change it."

**The poster** (`lib/crazydramas/poster.ts`; `POST /api/titles/[id]/crazydramas/poster`). Studio hosts it in a **public bucket of its own Supabase project, `public-posters`**, at `<title_external_id>/<sha8>.jpg`; crazydramas gets the bucket's public URL, `<NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL>/storage/v1/object/public/public-posters/<path>`.

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Source | JSON `{source: "cover"}`: the title's cover (`core.titles.cover_path`, read through the storage helpers; the import made it from `poster/final/`). Multipart with `file`: an image the person picked (at most 25 MB). JSON `{source: "url", url}`: a pasted address, checked by the poster check's rule and passed on as it is (not re-hosted). No cover: `409 poster_unavailable`; the form says so and creates the series without a poster. |
| Normalise | ffmpeg (`posterFfmpegArgs`; sharp is not a dependency): the first frame, `scale=1200:1600` when the source is already 3:4 (within 0.5%), else `scale=1200:1600:force_original_aspect_ratio=increase,crop=1200:1600` (cover-fit, centred crop, never letterboxed); `setsar=1`, `yuvj420p`, mjpeg `-q:v 3` (about quality 85), `-map_metadata -1` and `+bitexact` (no metadata, no encoder tag: the same source gives the same bytes, so the same name). The sha256 names the file and is returned with the URL. |
| Bucket | On the first poster of the process, `ensurePosterBucket` through the Storage API with the service role: read it; missing → create it public (`image/jpeg` only, 10 MB); private → make it public; a create that loses a race reads as there. Idempotent; remembered per process; a failure is forgotten, so the next poster tries again. Nobody runs SQL for it. |
| Store and check | The upload is content-addressed (`upsert`, a year's cache). Then the public URL must answer 200 `image/jpeg` (live: the poster check's HEAD through the transport, only to a named host whose every address is public) before anything goes on; else `poster_unreachable`. |
| Send | The form calls the route before its PUT and sends the `poster_url` it answered (the series PUT checks it again, as every poster is checked). With `apply` (the **Set poster** action) the route also sets it on the title's Studio series at once: `setSeriesPoster`, a PUT with `poster_url` alone, through the same guards as every write to an existing series (the link holds it, it is Studio's, no other title's; a CMS series is refused before any call); a series that is not a draft needs `confirm_live` (`409 series_live_confirm`) and then sends `update_live`. |

The form's poster field defaults to **the series' own poster** when it has one ("Keep the current poster"), else **the title's cover** (its preview beside the choice), else **none** ("This title has no cover…"); "Pick another image" and "Paste an address" are the other choices (`form_defaults.poster_default`, `has_cover`, `poster_preview_url`). **Fixture mode never reaches Supabase**: the bucket is `.uploads/public-posters/` (`fixturePosterStore`), served by `GET /api/public-posters/<title_external_id>/<sha8>.jpg` (fixture only; Supabase mode answers 404), under the made-up origin `https://studio-fixture.invalid` (the `.invalid` TLD resolves nowhere): the fake crazydramas stores that address, and `shownPosterUrl` / `fixturePosterPreview` show it through the same-origin route, so the browser still asks no other host.

**The slug** (`lib/crazydramas/slug.ts`, `lib/film-import/film-meta.ts`; `POST /api/titles/[id]/crazydramas/slug`). A film whose film-meta names no `crazydramas_slug` gets one on import (`slugOnImport`, before the index is read, so the `film_meta` asset carries it), and again when its upload form opens if the import could not reach crazydramas:

1. the show's own slug when it is already on the site: `KNOWN_LIVE_SERIES` (the working-name table) by title or folder, then the public catalog by title;
2. else `deriveSlug(display title)` (the folder's name when the title has nothing Latin in it): accents folded to ASCII, apostrophes inside a word dropped, `&` read as "and", every other run of marks one hyphen, lowercase, at most 80 characters cut at a word boundary (crazydramas' slug rule);
3. read on crazydramas (the authenticated Studio read, which sees drafts; the public read without a token or when the token is refused): **404** → free; **200 with the same title** (crazydramas' title key: case, spacing and punctuation ignored) that no other title's link holds → the show itself, linked read-only by the existing link rules (the check makes the link on its first 200 read); **200 with another series** → `-2`, `-3`, … (`slugCandidate`, the base cut back to stay within 80) until one is free, at most 30 tries (`slug_exhausted`).

The slug is saved on `core.titles.crazydramas_slug` and written into the film's `cut/film-meta.json`, Studio's one hand-writable file: every other key kept in its place (the slug goes after `display_title_en`), a minimal file that parses written when there is none, a file that is not a JSON object left exactly as it was, nothing else in the folder touched, the write a temp file and a rename. A read that fails is **`503 crazydramas_unreachable`**: nothing is saved, the import finishes with a warning (never a failure), and the form says "crazydramas did not answer…" with **Retry**. The form shows the slug as an editable field until the draft series exists: a typed slug is checked the same way, and one another series has is `409 slug_taken` with `suggestion` (the next free one, one click to take). Once a link exists or the ledger holds any upload the slug is **locked** (`409 slug_locked`; `form_defaults.slug_editable` false with `slug_locked_reason`): ad links point at crazydramas.com/watch/<slug>.

**The series text** (`lib/crazydramas/series-text.ts`, `lib/prompts/series-text.ts`; `POST /api/titles/[id]/crazydramas/series-text`). When the form opens for a title with no Studio series and its tagline, description or genres are empty, Studio drafts them from the film's transcript: the imported ASR index (`studio.film_assets` kind `transcript`, on the film's timeline), else the episodes' script lines laid end to end. `selectTranscript` gives the model every line that ends by min(15 minutes, 80% of the film) and up to 60 lines spaced evenly from there to the 80% mark: **never anything in the last 20%**, so the ending cannot reach the store copy. The answer (zod: a tagline of at most 80 characters in the catalog's voice, a 2–4 sentence spoiler-light description, 1–3 genres with the catalog's words first: Romance, Mafia, Billionaire, Secret Baby, Revenge, Campus, Sports, Betrayal, Paranormal, Mystery, Thriller) goes through `callStructured` on `ADS_TEXT_PROVIDER`'s fast tier as a `studio.jobs` row of kind `draft_series_text` (migration 0022) with its usage and cost, keyed `series_text:<prompt version>:<title>:<transcript sha16>:a<attempt>`: reopening the form reuses the newest draft of the same transcript; **Draft again** is the next attempt. A first draft fills only empty fields; Draft again replaces all three. The route answers the text alone, never the job or its cost. Demo replay (fixture mode) answers a canned draft (`status: demo`) and calls nothing; with no key for the provider, or no transcript, the fields stay empty with a one-line note (`status: unavailable`).

### What it never does

Publish on upload; write to a series made in the CMS; write anything from fixture mode but the fake; create a series whose show is already on the site; create a second Mux upload for a file whose upload is alive; send bytes from anywhere but the local-tier hardlink; send the last chunk after a CMS upload took the episode over; publish an episode that failed verification; delete anything (no Mux asset, upload or episode row); push; change crazydramas.
